# D4 hardware changelog

This log tracks released design-package versions inside the physical revision named in [`REVISION`](REVISION). Numbered engineering change notices carry the reason, evidence, and compatibility decision for each change.

## 0.1.4 - 2026-09-05

Added after the 0.1.4 body was released, additive and not superseding it:

- A **second body**, `hardware/cad/KINO_RESIN_BODY/`, printed entirely through JLCPCB, in three parts that separate the camera from its case ([`ECN-0005`](changes/ECN-0005-resin-body-skeleton-and-shell.md)). A **black MJF nylon skeleton** carries everything - four camera modules on the 22.00 mm optical datum, four XIAOs, the P4 module on the 61.9 x 54.8 standoff pattern with the same conical sockets, the loom - and is the only part with a thread. A **clear SLA shell**, 2.0 mm walls, is four walls and the rear frame: nothing electrical touches it and no tolerance depends on it, so it can be reprinted in another colour without re-qualifying an optical dimension. A **black tough-SLA face** carries the four lens cells. `KINO_FIELD_BODY` is untouched and stays the released body. The `cad` artifact goes to revision 2, and to revision 3 with the bench corrections below.
- The face is black and the shell is clear for an optical reason and not a cosmetic one: light entering a transparent wall travels inside it and reaches whatever the interior can see, so clear lens cells would put that light on the sensors and the firmware's cover-closed test - all four cameras dark means the cover is down - would never read dark. An opaque face makes the cells the baffle and an opaque skeleton blocks the path from inside; two gates probe a light path beside each cell on each part.
- The skeleton is nylon and not resin because it is the part that gets dropped: four camera modules, a display module and a strap load hang off it and standard SLA resin is brittle. It costs accuracy - MJF holds about ±0.3%, so the 66 mm span across the four cameras can be out by 0.2 mm where SLA would hold 0.1 - and that is the right trade, because the camera baseline is calibrated in software and a cracked camera mount is not calibrated out of anything. Nylon is also a thermoplastic, so it takes heat-set inserts where cured resin cannot: four M2.5 countersunk screws pass through the face into brass inserts in the skeleton, trapping the shell's front rim. No glue anywhere, no thread in any resin part, and the face comes off again - which the field body's permanently glued shell cannot do, and which is what created its "fit the sensor first or never" trap.
- MJF removes the support-free rule, so the skeleton deliberately has NO support-free gate: a powder bed has no supports to be free of, and carrying the field body's 45-degree rule would forbid geometry this process prints perfectly well. What it needs instead is powder escape and features coarse enough to resolve, which is why the pinch ribs are 0.5 mm proud against the field body's 0.20 - at 0.20 they would not have appeared.
- 125 x 82 x 65.5 mm against 131 x 90 x 65.5, and the saving is entirely in the walls: 2.0 mm of resin where PETG needed 6.0. Depth does not move relative to the field body, because depth is set by the electronics stack and not by any wall - a gate re-adds the stack every build and checks the module's PCB back lands on the same plane as the field body's, so the post and socket geometry transfers unchanged. Both were 64.6 x PCB back 45.6 until the camera's stand-off was derived rather than chosen (see the bench evidence below); both are now 65.5 x 46.5. Skeleton 34.54 cm^3, shell 50.13, face 30.55.
- 27 gates, 0 failures. There is NO independent scan on this package, and `promote.mjs` prints that absence every run rather than skipping it silently: the field body's `verify-release.mjs` arrived after 262 gates existed and immediately found an 870 mm^2 overhang and a blind spot every one of them had walked past, so until the equivalent exists here a pass is weaker evidence and a coupon comes before any trusted fit. Staged: the lens cover and its track, the door, the shutter, coupons, and that scan. The door zone at Z 60.6-64.6 is reserved and gated empty.
- Four defects the gates caught while building it, each a class of mistake rather than a one-off: a XIAO pad placed under the camera module and straight across the lens axis (the field body puts its pads above the module's top edge; copying the board's centreline instead put 3 mm of nylon in the light path); four insert pilots closed at both ends, which are four internal voids reported as four loose components of MINUS 56.6 mm^3 and four pockets of unsintered powder with no way out; pinch ribs unioned before their pocket was cut, so the cut left nothing standing; and bosses abutting the plate on a shared plane rather than embedded in it, leaving the union as five shells. Three of the four were found only because a gate reported WHERE rather than how many - the one-part gate now lists the loose pieces' own bounding boxes.

## Released in 0.1.4

Status: prototype. No physical unit has passed the acceptance sheet. The first body print failed on the bed.

**Bench evidence, 2026-09-08.** Several parts of this version have now been printed and five faults reported off them. All five are answered below.

1. **The P4 posts miss the standoffs, and they SPLAY** - too far apart or too close, not all shifted the same way. Answered below: the 65.5 x 58.2 was an OUTSIDE SPAN written into a centre-pitch field.
2. **Both slides move freely and feel gritty** - not tight. Answered below; the clearances were deliberately NOT touched.
3. **The camera cannot reach its lens hole on the wiggle rig**, because the ribbon is too short - about 7 mm - from where the XIAO sits. Answered below: the rig's board stand-off was a chosen 7.0 mm and the camera stands 7.4 mm off the board face, so the lens stopped about 2 mm short of its pocket.
4. **The shutter button does not account for the switch's attachment points, or for leads leaving at OPPOSITE ends.** Answered below.
5. The wiggle rig is otherwise sound and the XIAO stack height is confirmed.

- The P4 pattern goes back to **61.9 x 54.8 mm centre pitch**, and the splay is explained rather than re-measured: 65.5 x 58.2 was a span read across the OUTSIDE EDGES of the standoffs and written into a field that means centre-to-centre. 61.9 + 3.5 = 65.4 and 54.8 + 3.5 = 58.3, which is the reported 65 x 58 to within a caliper's parallax - the same holes, described from a different datum. That also explains the shape of the fault: a wrong offset shifts all four posts the same way, and only a wrong pitch spreads them, which is what "splay" means. The two figures can no longer disagree silently: a gate in both generators requires `pitch + standoffOD` to equal the measured outside span within 0.5 mm, so an edge reading entered as a pitch fails the build. The knock-ons of the wrong pattern reverse with it - the loom's tightest raceway returns from 52 to 95 mm² and all four nodes take a straight USB plug again.
- **The camera's stand-off is derived now, and that is the whole fix.** Two photographs of the assembled module settled it: the camera's FPC connector is on the boards' OUTER face - the same face the module folds back onto over its own 7 mm ribbon - so the module lies flat on the stack, its lens looks along the boards' normal, and it has no second position to be in. Seeed give the assembled stack as 21 x 17.8 x **15 mm**, and that 15.0 runs from the XIAO's back face to the LENS's front face with the fold and the connector already inside it. So the stand-off that lands the lens on the pocket floor is `15.0 - 7.6 of boards - 3.5 of pocket = 3.9 mm`. The body declared 3.0 and the rig declared 7.0 - the rig's 7.0 "set generously enough that the module and the fold fit under the board whatever the module turns out to measure", which is the comment of somebody who did not have this figure. Generous was the fault. Body depth 64.6 -> 65.5 mm, front half 44.3 -> 45.2 mm tall, 135.07 -> 138.00 cm3, and the rig now takes the body's derived figure so that what the rig proves about reach is what the body does.
- The gate that existed for exactly this failure - "camera head reaches into its pocket" - passed the rig, and it is worth saying why, because the shape of the mistake recurs: it compared a PLAUSIBLE head height of 4-6 mm against a DECLARED stand-off of 3.0, so it could only ever confirm that two guesses agreed with each other. Neither had been measured. It is replaced by five checks on the vendor's chain, each term measured or derived from something measured: the board's back face less the stack's overall height IS the lens face, and that plane has to be the pocket floor; the module's PCB has somewhere flat to seat with the fold behind it; the head fits inside the plate; the field cone's pupil is not in front of the lens; and the fold costs less ribbon than there is. `camModuleHeight` is deleted rather than corrected - a plausible range is not an input.
- The stack coupon's stations go from 3/5/7/9 mm to half-millimetre steps bracketing the derived 3.9, because its job changed from searching a range to checking one figure. The old spread is part of the evidence: a coupon offering 3 and 7 as equally plausible is a coupon whose author did not know the answer to within 4 mm, and the rig was built at 7.
- The resin body takes the same 3.9 mm, since the chain from the board to the lens belongs to the camera and not to the body. Its skeleton plate is only 2.5 mm thick against the field body's 5.0, so the head passes through the plate's bore and 1.0 mm on into the face's lens cell, which is open air behind its bore. Resin depth 64.6 -> 65.5, skeleton 42.7 mm deep and 34.54 cm3, shell 50.13.
- The vent slots' Z is derived from the chassis cut now, not declared. At a declared 33.0 with the cut at 31.0 they had 2.0 mm of margin, and moving the module plane 0.9 mm back spent all of it: the slots' gable points reached over the joint and the gate caught them in the wrong half. What a slot needs is a stated clearance from the cut, not a Z of its own.
- The shutter switch was modelled as a plain 6 x 6 x 4.3 block with both leads leaving the same face, and neither is true of the switch fitted: its terminals stand proud of the body, and the two leads leave at opposite ends. So the retainer's cradle - a 6.6 mm square socket with ONE notch in it - was clamping the terminals, and one lead had nowhere to run but back across the switch. Three changes. The cradle gets a **terminal slot** through both side walls at the switch's base plane, 4.6 mm wide and 1.8 mm tall, with a 0.6 mm dish in the stop plate under it for legs bent back rather than out; the slot is deliberately NOT sized to a leg span, because run clean through the wall it passes terminals of any span up to the cup's Ø14.4 while the 1 mm corner lands each side still locate the switch. The cradle gains a **second wire notch** opposite the first, since the retainer is a round plate the assembler can seat at any rotation and relieving one side only worked if that was the side the lead was on. And the body gets **one lead channel off each end of the switch pocket** - Ø2 lead in a 3.0 mm gabled channel to the open bay - beside the existing pair under the switch. Sideways rather than above: a mirrored channel above the switch would put its gable apex at Z 31.2, through the chassis cut at 31.0 and into the rear half's bed face, where sideways it stays inside the pod's 19 mm width with 6 mm to spare. 4.6 mm rather than the cradle's full 6.6 keeps the slot's roof a bridge the layer gate allows; measured on the shipped button, widest bridge 3.90 mm. Two new gates probe a lead out of each end of the pocket into the bay, and two more probe out through both cradle walls at the base plane and check the stop plate still has 1.0 mm under the dish. Reprint `coupons/KINO_SHUTTER_COUPON.stl` before the front half. The leg BAND height, 1.8 mm, is the one figure here taken from the 6 x 6 tactile family rather than from a caliper on the switch fitted, and it is marked PROVISIONAL in the source.
- 385 gates, 0 failures; 82 independent checks ALL CLEAR. Resin body 27 gates, 0 failures.

- Both sliding fits get BEARING SURFACES rather than a different clearance, because "moves freely but gritty" is a surface fault and not a fit one. Loosening them again would only have brought back the rattle the first coupon had at 0.3 mm. The cover rode its whole back - 89.4 x 20.4 mm - on the panel floor, and for the last 21.4 mm of travel that floor is the stow hatch: 0.6 mm grooves at 45 degrees on a 2.5 mm pitch. The comment beside the hatch said so in as many words - "the cover still rides on the floor's ridges when it is down" - and nobody had read it as a fault. Sliding a flat face across a diagonal comb is gritty by construction. The floor is now relieved 0.25 mm across the cover's whole swept band with two 2.5 mm lands left standing at X 36 and 96, and the hatch is kept 1.25 mm clear of each: contact falls from about 1824 mm^2 of hatched floor to 102 mm^2 of smooth land, and every remaining rub runs parallel to the travel.
- Put on the COVER first, as two rails on its back, and that is exactly wrong: the cover prints back-down, so its back is the bed face, and a 0.25 mm recess leaves 688 mm^2 of plate floating a quarter of a millimetre above the bed. The layer gate caught it as an island. The relief belongs in the floor, which is an upward-facing surface on a part that prints relief-up, where a recess opens upward and costs nothing - and because the lands stay at the original floor plane, not one Z datum in the cover, the keeper, the lips or the detent moves.
- The relief and the hatch were then fighting for the same 0.3 mm: cut from the original plane the hatch read 86% removed against 24% intended, because the relief had already taken 0.25 of the 0.30 being sampled. The hatch is cut from the RELIEVED plane now, and both hatch gates sample from it.
- The door's tongue bore against the shoulder along the whole 118 mm of the door, and that shoulder is the one surface on this camera known to be rough - a 1.5 mm one-sided bridge, the largest unsupported reach on the part, sagging a tenth on its first line. Dragging a flat tongue along 118 mm of sagged bridge is the door's version of the cover riding its hatch. The tongue's top is dropped 0.25 mm with four 10 mm pads left standing, so the load is carried at four known places instead of wherever the bridge happened to droop least, and the 0.35 mm of Z clearance still swallows the sag.
- Built by subtracting a skimmed box from a full-height tongue first, which leaves slivers where the pad walls meet the tongue's old top plane: one degenerate triangle that appeared only on plate 2, after the translate re-rounded it in float32. Built additively instead, with the pads inset 0.05 mm in Y so their side walls do not share a plane with the tongue's - the abutting-faces fault, for the fourth time in this design.
- Face shell 53.86 cm^3, door 12.25, plate 2 74.80, track coupon 26.41, door-joint coupon 14.88.

## Released in 0.1.4

- The field body is a plain rounded slab, 131 x 90 x 64.6 mm, in place of the Nishika-styled 147 x 115 x 68 mm chassis. The grip block, stepped top plate, prism hump, thumb rest, angled band and grip drum are gone - 126 cm^3, 36% of the chassis, carried for the look - after the maintainer supplied reference slab cameras. Width is set by the P4 module plus two 6 mm walls; height by the module plus the shutter pod's reach.
- The chassis prints as two parts cut at the bay floor: front 131 x 92.2 x 44.3 mm (135.1 cm^3, the whole optical datum) and rear 137.5 x 92.2 x 33.6 mm (78.3 cm^3 with the strap anchor, module seat and door). Four M2.5 x 16 along Z in wall pads at X 18 and 92, coaxial Ø4.6 pegs for alignment, heads reached from the door past a fitted module. Longest single print falls from 9.2 h to 6.2 h at 0.20 mm.
- **Superseded 2026-09-08, kept for the reasoning** - the pitch below was an outside-edge span and the pattern is back at 61.9 x 54.8; see the bench-evidence block above. The standoff DIAMETER and HEIGHT re-measured here, Ø3.5 x 3.3, stand. The P4 standoff pattern re-measures at 65.5 x 58.2 of Ø3.5 x 3.3 brass, against the 61.9 x 54.8 of Ø5.4 x 3.0 this record called caliper-confirmed. Every post moves 1.8 mm outward in X and 1.7 in Y and the socket floor drops 0.3 mm, so the old pattern would have given a body the module could not be seated in at all. The front half is 44.3 mm tall rather than 44.6 and the post rim clears the PCB by 1.3 mm rather than 1.0. Two knock-ons, recorded rather than designed around: the loom's tightest raceway falls from 95 to 52 mm² against the 45 it needs, and two of the four nodes now want a right-angle USB plug for in-place reflashing rather than one. The pattern centre's 9.3 mm offset is the only figure of the group still unmeasured; the paper template is now there to check it.
- The P4 module's brass standoffs now drop into sockets in the posts (see the cone entry below; straight Ø5.9 then Ø5.7 in earlier cuts) instead of resting on flat post faces with 0.3 mm of float. Foam against the door still provides the Z preload; the foam must be thicker than the 1.2 mm gap. The bridge gauge's pads carry the same socket, so its first print is the bore's fit test.
- The door prints INNER-face-down, not outer-face-down, and that is what actually fixes the dovetail. Steepening the ceiling to 55 degrees (below) was necessary and not sufficient: the wedge's section is a Z - full width at the door's inner face, narrowing to the lip, flaring back out to the deep face - and printed outer-face-down that Z is upside down, so the wedge's outermost tip arrived 1.5 mm above the bed with NOTHING BENEATH IT. Measured on the shipped STL: 36.9 mm^2 of material appearing out of air in a single layer, 0.147 mm wide along the whole 125 mm of both edges, seeding a 337 mm^2 dovetail off a line the printer had to lay in space. No angle fixes that, and every overhang gate passed it - in the generator and in the independent scan - because all of them asked how STEEP a downward face was and none asked whether its lowest edge had anything to land on. The maintainer found it in the slicer, twice, and was right twice. Turned over, the same section sits on the bed at full width and tapers upward: the dovetail has no downward face at all, and the surface that mates the groove is now referenced to the bed, which is the best any face on the part can be. Islands on the door go from 36.9 mm^2 to zero and bridges from 45.7 mm^2 to 2.2; the widest bridge falls from 1.48 mm to 0.04.
- Three things fall out of turning the door over. The serial engraved in the outer face stops being a recess roof on the bed - 24.9 mm^2 of it - and becomes an upward-opening recess; the window's outer bevel becomes an upward cone; the finger scallop opens upward. What it costs is the show face, which was bed-glass and is now the print's top surface, so the door's outer face carries the face plate's grain: the same 45-degree 0.5 mm grooves on a 1.6 mm pitch, clipped clear of the outline, of the window and its 2 mm bevel, and of the serial. The camera now reads as one finish across the walls, the face plate and the door rather than three.
- New gate, and the one that should always have existed: **every part builds layer on layer**. It slices each released part in its own print orientation at the released layer height and requires every layer's material to be within reach of the layer beneath it, reach being one layer height at 45 degrees. It then splits what is left into two kinds, because one number cannot judge both: a BRIDGE has supported material beside it in the same layer (a bore roof, the top of an engraved letter) and is held to the 5 mm span this design already uses; an ISLAND has nothing under it and nothing beside it, and is held to two limits - never as wide as one bead, because narrower than that the slicer drops it and the feature merely comes out rounded (the dovetail groove's mouth does exactly this at 0.06 mm), and never more than 20 mm^2 in a single layer, because that is not a stray sliver but the start of a feature. Verified against the defect it exists for: it fails the old door at 36.9 mm^2 and passes the new one with zero islands. All eight printed parts are island-free, widest bridge 1.48 mm on the cover's lettering.
- `bezel prints support-free` drops its per-triangle area limit, replaced by the span in the layer gate. Triangle area was standing in for unsupported span, and on the door that proxy breaks: the detent relief notch's roof is 0.2 mm wide and 110 mm long, so it reads as 11.6 mm^2 of area and is a 0.2 mm bridge - narrower than the nozzle that crosses it, in a channel whose whole purpose is clearance. Area cannot tell that from a 3 x 4 mm hole roof; span can.
- **The dovetail is gone.** The slide is a square-shouldered tongue: a 1.5 mm tall rectangular tongue on each door edge running in a 1.85 mm rectangular groove, with a flat shoulder over it. The dovetail was tried at 47 degrees and then at 55, and the angle was never the problem - the mechanism was wrong for these two print orientations, and the argument is one line: the foam pushes the door OUTWARD, so the body's retaining surface must face -Z; the rear half prints cut-face-down, so -Z is DOWN; and a SLOPED retaining surface therefore always begins as an unanchored knife edge. Measured layer by layer on the shipped rear half: at Z 30.60 the only material was the wall at Y 85.51+, at Z 30.80 a 0.11 mm ledge appeared at Y 84.00 with 1.4 mm of air beside it and nothing beneath it, and it grew for ELEVEN layers before meeting the wall at Z 32.80 - a 118 mm long, 2.2 mm tall unsupported flag on each wall. The same defect the door had, and the maintainer called it on both.
- A square shoulder is strictly better here, which is the counter-intuitive part. Its roof appears in ONE layer at its full 1.5 mm width, touching the groove's deep wall in that same layer - material that runs solid all the way down - so it is a one-sided 1.5 mm bridge off a root that exists, like the roof of any counterbore, instead of a cantilever grown from nothing. Re-probed after the change: the groove is open for seven layers and the shoulder closes complete in one. On the door the tongue becomes a plain two-step plan, full width on the bed and narrowing once, so the door now measures ZERO unsupported material of any kind - no islands and no bridges at all. The rear half keeps 340 mm^2 of bridges (the shoulder) and no islands. What the dovetail was for, resisting the door lifting under preload, the foam does not need: a square shoulder simply stops it, and a slope under load only cams the door sideways. Retention is complete because both tongues are captured - the door moves in X and nowhere else.
- Three angle-based gates had to give way to the layer gate, and this is worth being explicit about because it looks like a relaxation and is not. By ANGLE a flat anchored ledge scores worse than the 47-degree cantilever it replaced: the rear half's `>45deg downward` goes from 100 to 455 mm^2, of which about 354 is the shoulder. So the rear half's budget goes 400 -> 700 in the generator and in the independent scan, the door-joint coupon's 80 -> 200, and the per-triangle limits come off the coupon and door gates entirely - because triangle area was standing in for unsupported SPAN and cannot tell a 1.5 x 118 mm anchored ledge from a cantilever in mid-air. The layer gate can, and it is the authority for these parts now. The detent rib drops 2.1 -> 1.5 mm to match the tongue; at 2.1 its top 0.45 mm was buried inside the shoulder, harmless as a union but a number nobody meant.
- The layer gate's island test was itself wrong on its first cut and is rewritten. It asked, per connected piece, "does this touch anchored material anywhere?" - useless on a long feature, because the rear half's ledge touches the wall at its very top and so the whole 118 mm was scored as a bridge, reporting zero islands on a part that was 2.2 mm of flag. Euclidean distance is no better: it cannot tell an island sitting 1.4 mm from a wall from a bridge 1.4 mm wide. It now grows the anchored material outward THROUGH THIS LAYER'S OWN MATERIAL, one 0.3 mm step at a time; what is never reached has no path back to anything anchored and is an island however close it sits, and material reached only beyond 2.5 mm of path has over-reached its anchor. Verified against both defects it exists for: it fails the old door at 36.9 mm^2 and the old rear half at 9.1 mm^2, and passes the rebuilt pair with zero islands and nothing over-reaching.
- `coupons/KINO_SHUTTER_COUPON.stl` (77.6 x 19.3 x 23 mm, 6.7 cm^3, three parts): a chunk of the front half around X 115 carrying the recessed collar, the access bore, the pod pilaster, the switch pocket and the wire exit, in the front half's own print orientation, plus both button parts beside it. The shutter is the only thing a user operates besides the cover and the door, and it was the only one of the three with no coupon - so every fit in it between a printed part and a bought 6 x 6 x 4.3 mm switch was going to be tried for the first time on the camera itself. Answers whether the stem slides without binding, whether the retainer holds the actuator captive, whether the press travel closes the switch without bottoming the stem, and whether the leads get out. Gated for its features as well as its size: three pieces, and the stem path through the wall and pod open end to end.
- Z clearance on the slide goes 0.15 -> 0.35 mm while side clearance stays at 0.15, and the asymmetry is the point: on this joint Z clearance is FREE, because the foam presses the door outward against the shoulder and takes up whatever gap is drawn. So it is spent on the shoulder's droop. Measured, that shoulder is the largest unsupported event on the camera - one layer, 310 mm^2 reaching 1.4 mm from anchored material, continuous over 118 mm on each wall, against the 1.0 mm of the light bore's roof which is the worst thing on the front half. It will print: a 1.4 mm one-sided ledge in PETG at 0.2 mm layers is ordinary hole-roof work. It will also sag a tenth or so on that first line, and at 0.15 mm of gap a tenth of sag was most of the fit. Costs 0.2 mm of wall over the shoulder, 2.35 -> 2.15, against the 2.0 its gate requires.
- Rear half 78.10 cm^3, door 12.30, plate 1 213.2, plate 2 75.45.
- Superseded, kept for the reasoning: the door dovetail was steepened from 47 to 55.1 degrees from horizontal, and the bite dropped from 2.0 to 1.5 mm to do it (the 2.15 mm rise stayed). The groove ceiling in the rear half and the wedge's top face on the door are the same surface seen from two sides, and both are unavoidable DOWNWARD faces: the rear half prints cut-face-down and the door outer-face-down, so each leans out over air for the door's whole 118 mm. At 47 degrees that was 688 mm^2 on the rear half and 730 on the door - the four largest downward faces on either part, the shallowest large surface anywhere on the camera, and inside a slicer's overhang shading, which is where the maintainer found it. 47 was chosen as "comfortably past the 45-degree rule"; the rule this design actually uses for an unavoidable downward face is FIFTY, as the window bevel and the seam chamfer both do, because at a nominal 45 the slice epsilons produced 44.5 and 426 mm^2 of the seam read as overhang. After the change the door has nothing at all shallower than 48 degrees and the rear half's shallowest large face is a foot chamfer. Costs 0.5 mm of dovetail engagement a side, still more than the 1.2 mm lips that hold the lens cover; buys 0.5 mm more web between the groove and the cable groove in the same wall, so that gate reads 2.5 mm rather than 2.0. Keeping the bite at 2.0 and raising the rise to 2.86 was the other route to 55, and it would have left 0.14 mm of wall over the groove. Both slopes are now derived from one pair of parameters, because a wedge cut to a different angle than its groove either binds or rattles and nothing else in the assembly would say which. Reprint the door-joint coupon before the rear half.
- The rear door's clearances drop from 0.3 to 0.15 mm a side and under the groove ceiling, and its detent is rebuilt: the first cut's bump on the wedge stopped 0.05 mm short of the groove wall and its holding gate was reading the resting film. Now a Ø1.0 vertical rib on each groove wall stands 0.30 mm proud; the wedge carries a relief notch, ramp, 2 mm crest and dimple, so the door slides free and clicks in the last 2.5 mm. Probed by a needle gate at the rib tip, in the generator and in the independent scan. Print the door-joint coupon before the rear half.
- The face shell's lens cells are set out from a 78-degree diagonal field cone - a design figure for the OV3660 modules fitted, whose actual field is `MEASURE_REQUIRED` in `docs/HARDWARE.md` - instead of fixed diameters. The old cells passed 70 degrees and put the shell in every frame; the new Ø18.4 cells pass 81.9 on all four lenses, measured with the lens cover open.
- A vertical sliding lens cover, 89.4 x 20.4 x 2 mm, runs in the lens bar's recessed panel under dovetail lips and is bistable on one pair of Ø0.8 vertical ribs on the track walls, met by 1.5 mm crests at each end of the cover's edges with a relief between (the first detent, spheres in a 0.5 mm gap against R2 corners, never touched the cover): closed over the cells (100% coverage), open BELOW them - it drops to shoot, 21.4 mm. Clearances 0.1 mm a side, 0.2 mm deep, 0.10 mm of forward play. The accent ledge runs along the panel's top and bottom only so the lips are backed by the full side rim (as a frame it left each lip's top 0.8 mm as a one-bead fin). It enters through a gap in the bar's bottom rim, and `KINO_FIELD_SLIDER_KEEPER` (89.4 x 5.5 x 2.2 mm) is glued into that gap behind it as the stop. The first cut (stowing upward, 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.17 mm, two exit bumps for a stop) was printed as a coupon and rattled, fell out and opened the wrong way. It carries the KINO D4 wordmark, raised 0.6 mm, traced from the brand raster to smooth vector outlines (12 polygons, 472 vertices at 80 x 10.7 mm: upsampled to 0.0175 mm, contours smoothed over 0.16 mm, simplified to 0.01; a 0.45 mm pixel-grid trace and a 0.07 mm pixel-edge trace were both rejected as faceted; the trace lives under the brand's reserved terms and is read at build time). The face carries no other lettering.

## 0.1.3 - 2026-08-30

Status: prototype. No physical unit has passed the acceptance sheet.

- Gave `BTN_SHUTTER` a pin: `GPIO28`, JP1 pin 21, which 0.1.2 had assigned to `FLASH_EN`. The body needs a shutter and JP1 had no other pin that is safe to switch to ground. `GPIO35` on pin 15 is the serial-bootloader strap and stays unconnected; `GPIO31` on pin 10 keeps `CAM_PWR_EN`, because that line is idle power-down for the camera bank and the only way the P4 can power-cycle a hung node. `GPIO28` is not a strapping pin - 34 to 38 are.
- Dropped the built-in constant-current direct-flash assembly from D4-V1. A separate external flash module will be used and has no P4 pin in this revision; its interface is not chosen. `FLASH_EN` was an output that had never been asserted into a board that had never been built.
- The switch is a 6 x 6 x 4.3 mm tactile part between JP1 21 and GND (pin 6 or pin 16), with no external resistor and no debounce capacitor: the P4's internal pull-up holds the pin high and firmware debounces 25 ms.
- Twelve header GPIOs still carry eleven signals: the four TX/RX pairs, `SYNC_OUT` (GPIO32, pin 19), `CAM_PWR_EN` (GPIO31, pin 10) and `BTN_SHUTTER` (GPIO28, pin 21). `BTN_FN` and `SLIDE_MODE` remain unassigned.
- `GPIO_TBD` stays in WIRING.md and `finalGpioMap` stays false: the header position is measured, but nobody has pressed the button on a board yet.

Engineering change notice: [`ECN-0003`](changes/ECN-0003-shutter-on-jp1-21.md).
