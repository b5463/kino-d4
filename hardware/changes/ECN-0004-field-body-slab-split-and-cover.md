## Problem

The first print of `KINO_FIELD_BODY_PRINT.stl` failed: the slicer generated supports where the part needs none, infill broke up in places, the nozzle dragged through it and knocked the supports over. The part was 147 × 115.2 × 67.8 mm and 345.5 cm³, styled after a Nishika N8000 with a grip block, a stepped top plate and a prism hump, and a 9 h single print on a machine that has already lost one is a bad bet regardless of why this one failed.

Bench measurements taken for this change also showed the design carried assumptions that were wrong, several of them invisible in renders:

- The lens cells in the face shell were set out from fixed diameters (Ø14 / Ø11.5 / Ø7.1) and left 5.06 mm of parallel Ø7.1 bore in front of the entrance pupil. That passes 70°; the design field for the OV3660 modules fitted is 78° diagonal. **The shell was in every frame.**
- The rear door's window was 94.0 × 57.0 over a 93.6 × 56.16 active area: 0.20 mm per side in X, against a module that can sit 0.25 mm off centre on its standoffs. The door would have clipped the picture.
- The P4 module was held only by foam pressure against a slide-in door, with 0.3 mm of plan float and no positive location.
- Every raised label was laid out mirrored in body coordinates, and read correctly on a printed part only because the face shell was written with a Z reflection whose mirror image cancelled the layout error once the part was flipped onto the camera.
- The chassis' own support-free gate excluded any triangle *touching* the bed plane, so a chamfer on a bed face — an overhang by definition — was invisible to it.

## Proposed change

All geometry is in `hardware/cad/KINO_FIELD_BODY/generate-field-body.mjs`; every number below is measured off the released meshes by the release report or by `verify-release.mjs`.

**Form.** The N8000 pastiche is gone: grip block (88.1 cm³), stepped top plate (31.0), prism hump (7.0), thumb rest, angled band, grip drum and its ribs, the `D4`/`V1` marks. The chassis is one rounded slab, **131 × 90 × 65.5 mm** (64.6 until the camera's stand-off was derived rather than chosen — see below), matching the reference slab cameras the maintainer supplied. 131 is set by the P4 module (117.01 + two 6 mm walls = 129); 90 is set by the module's height plus the shutter pod's reach into the cavity, measured at 89.4 minimum.

**Two prints, cut at the bay floor (Z = 31.0).** Front half 131 × 92.2 × 44.3 mm, 135.07 cm³; rear half 135.5 × 92.2 × 33.6 mm, 77.74 cm³ with the strap lug (the 92.2 includes 2.2 mm of feet). The entire optical datum — plate, camera pockets, lens bores, XIAO seats and fences, the 22 mm pitch — is in the front half; the rear half carries only the module seat and the door. Joint: four **M2.5 × 16** socket-head bolts along Z in pads on the inside of the top and bottom walls at X 18 and 92, each with a coaxial Ø4.6 × 3 mm peg into a Ø4.9 socket. The bolt axis sits 1.25 mm inside the wall face; the head pocket is open-sided to the cavity and reached from the door through a 1.45 mm-deep channel in the wall's inner face. A Ø2.5 driver clears a fitted module by 1.80 mm, so the halves separate with only the door off. No rabbet: a rabbet leaves half the wall hanging 3 mm above the bed on whichever half prints cut-face-down.

**P4 module capture, on a re-measured pattern.** *Corrected 2026-09-08 after the first bench print: the pattern is back at **61.9 × 54.8 centre pitch**. The 65.5 × 58.2 below was a span read across the standoffs' OUTSIDE EDGES and written into a field that means centre-to-centre — 61.9 + 3.5 = 65.4 and 54.8 + 3.5 = 58.3, the same four holes from a different datum — and it is why the printed posts splayed rather than all shifting one way. The re-measured DIAMETER and HEIGHT, Ø3.5 × 3.3, stand, and so does everything below that follows from them. Both generators now gate `pitch + standoffOD` against the measured outside span within 0.5 mm, so the two datums cannot disagree silently again.* The standoff group was recorded as a 61.9 × 54.8 pattern of Ø5.4 × 3.0 brass and described as caliper-confirmed. All three figures were wrong. Measured on the board it is **65.5 × 58.2 of Ø3.5 × 3.3**, which moves every post 1.8 mm outward in X and 1.7 in Y and drops the socket floor 0.3 mm. The old pattern would have produced a body the module simply could not be seated in, and it was found only because the diameter error prompted re-measuring the whole group rather than patching one number. The front half is consequently 44.3 mm tall rather than 44.6, the post rim now stops 1.3 mm short of the PCB instead of 1.0, and the paper template's purpose has narrowed to the one figure still unmeasured: the pattern centre's 9.3 mm offset from module centre, which is now the only way the posts can still be in the wrong place. Two consequences of the posts moving outward, both recorded rather than designed around: the Dupont loom's tightest raceway falls from 95 mm² to 52 against the 45 it needs, and a 0.45 mm sliver of node 4's USB plug envelope is clipped, so two of the four nodes want a compact right-angle plug for in-place reflashing rather than one. The four Ø8 posts carry conical sockets: Ø4.20 at the mouth closing to Ø3.60 at the floor over 2.0 mm (0.35 / 0.05 mm a side), 1.9 mm of post wall around the mouth. Two straight bores preceded it — Ø5.90, which gave the module 0.5 mm of plan float until the foam pinned it, and Ø5.70 — and the maintainer asked for a tighter fit with a lead-in; a cone is what a printer reproduces, since the first tenth of a straight bore decides a press fit and a printer misses it by its own tolerance. Gated with three probes per socket (Ø3.48 fits to the floor, Ø3.70 touches the first 0.3 mm, Ø4.00 clears the top 0.3 mm). The bridge gauge's pads carry the same cone instead of a scribed ring, so no press fit goes untested. The socket floor is at the old post-face plane, so the PCB back stays on the module plane the depth stack derives (45.6 then, 46.5 now that the camera's stand-off is derived). The post rim stops 1.3 mm short of the board. Foam preload against the door holds Z; the sockets locate plan. No screws enter the module.

**Lens cells derived from the field cone.** The face shell's cells are set out from a 78° diagonal field plus 2° margin from a pupil assumed 2.6 mm behind the plate face (worst case: barrel not protruding). Cells are Ø18.4 at the panel floor; the assembled shells pass **81.9°**, identical on all four lenses, and a gate fuses the *open* lens cover into the assembly before measuring.

**Sliding lens cover.** Vertical, over the cells only: a sideways slider needs 169 mm of face for four lenses spanning 84.4 mm. The lens bar is Y 3.9–60.7 and its recessed panel (2.2 deep, Y 11.1–53.5) is the track, with dovetail lips 1.2 mm deep on both side walls (underside 50°), backed by the full 4.7 mm side rim (the accent ledge runs along the panel's top and bottom only; as a frame it left each lip's top 0.8 mm standing as a one-bead fin over the ledge's air, which the independent thin-feature scan found). The cover is 89.4 × 20.4 × 2.0 mm with matching 45° edge bevels, R0.6 plan corners, and sits 0.2 mm below the bar face. **It drops to shoot**: closed at Y 32.8–53.2 (hiding 100 % of every cell mouth), open at Y 11.4–31.8 (clear of every cell, top edge 2.0 mm under the cell bottoms), 21.4 mm of travel. Detent: a Ø0.8 vertical rib on each track wall at Y 32.3, standing 0.8 mm on the track floor in the 1.0 mm between the positions, sunk so exactly sliderClr + 0.15 stands proud; the cover's side edges carry a 1.5 mm crest at each end, a 0.6 mm ramp and a 0.2 mm relief between, so the crest clicks over the ribs leaving either position and the ribs ride free for the 18 mm between. (The first version was two Ø1.2 spheres in a 0.5 mm gap against R2 corners: the corner swept past the sphere and the "runs free" gate passed because nothing ever touched; found by the scan's needle probes, and re-gated four ways.) Clearances: 0.1 mm a side, 0.2 mm of depth, 0.10 mm of forward play before the bevel meets the lip. The cover enters from below through a gap in the bar's bottom rim; once in, **`KINO_FIELD_SLIDER_KEEPER.stl`** (89.4 × 5.5 × 2.2 mm, 0.9 cm³ — the piece of rim the gap removed, 0.1 mm smaller on its fitted sides) is glued into the gap as a positive stop: a cover pushed 1.5 mm past open meets 136 mm³ of it.

This is the second cut of the cover. The first stowed *above* the cells (bar Y 24.5–84, track 31.7–76.8), had 0.3 mm a side, 0.4 mm of depth clearance and 0.17 mm of forward play, and relied on a second pair of bumps at the entry to keep the cover in. The maintainer printed its coupon: the cover rattled in the track, nothing stopped it leaving the open end, and it opened the wrong way for a camera held at the eye. Everything about the track that a coupon can test was changed in answer; the coupon was recut to carry all of it (see below).

**Door window** opened to **105 × 61.4** with a 2.0 mm flare at 50°: frame even at 10.0 / 10.3 mm per side, 5.7 / 2.6 mm per side of the panel's own dead border visible, 6.0 / 4.0 mm of module lip kept.

**Door fit and detent, second cut.** Found by reading the door against the cover coupon's findings: it was built to the same clearances (0.3 mm a side, 0.3 under the groove ceiling), and its detent did not engage — a Ø0.9 bump on the wedge stopped 0.05 mm short of the groove wall over a 0.15 mm dimple, and the gate that reported it holding (0.087 mm³) was measuring the resting film, which the free-travel positions also show at 0.03–0.07. Now: 0.15 mm a side and 0.15 under the ceiling, the wedge's lip height derived from that; the detent turned round into a Ø1.0 vertical rib on each groove's deep wall, standing 2.1 mm on the groove floor (a pillar in print, no overhang), 12 mm in from the entry, sunk so 0.30 mm stands proud (0.15 clearance + 0.15 bite), with the wedge carrying a 0.2 mm relief notch open toward the door's inner face (no print ceiling) from the leading end to 2.5 mm short of the seat, a 0.8 mm ramp, a 2 mm crest of full bite and a 0.25 mm dimple. The gate is a needle at the bump's tip with four yes/no answers (in the wall, in the dimple seated, in the crest 1.5 mm off, in free air 20 mm off). The door-joint coupon carries all of it.

**The camera has to reach its lens hole.** *Added 2026-09-08 from the first bench print, where it did not.* The camera is a separate module on a ribbon, and the ribbon decides everything: it is **7 mm** long, its FPC connector is on the boards' **outer** face — the same face the module folds back onto — so the module lies flat on the stack, its lens looks along the boards' normal, and it has no second position to be in. Seeed give the assembled stack as 21 × 17.8 × **15 mm**, and that 15.0 runs from the XIAO's back face to the **lens's front face**, fold and connector already inside it. The board stand-off is therefore not a free parameter: `15.0 − 7.6 of boards − 3.5 of pocket = 3.9 mm`. The body declared **3.0** and the wiggle rig declared **7.0**, the rig's on the argument that a bench rig has no depth budget so generous was safe — and generous is the fault: at 7.0 the lens stopped about 2 mm short of its pocket and the rig's cameras could not see out. `xiaoPadH` is derived in both bodies now and the rig reads it, so what the rig proves about reach is what the body does. Costs 0.9 mm of body depth (64.6 → 65.5); the front half goes 44.3 → 45.2 mm tall and 135.07 → 138.00 cm³.

The gate that existed for this failure passed it, and the shape of that mistake matters more than the millimetre: **"camera head reaches into its pocket" compared a plausible 4–6 mm head height against a declared 3.0 mm stand-off** — two figures nobody had measured — so it could only ever confirm that two guesses agreed with each other. It is replaced by five checks on the vendor's chain, every term measured or derived from something measured: the board's back face less the stack's overall height IS the lens face and that plane must be the pocket floor; the module's PCB seats flat with the fold behind it; the head fits inside the plate; the field cone's pupil is not in front of the lens; and the fold costs less ribbon than there is. `camModuleHeight` is deleted rather than corrected, because a plausible range is not an input. `camPocketDepth` becomes derived too — as deep as the plate can give while leaving 1.5 mm to carry the bore — since the head's split into holder and barrel is unmeasured and a pocket sized to the holder can bottom early, where a pocket sized to the plate cannot. The stack coupon's stations go from 3/5/7/9 mm to half-millimetre steps around 3.9: its job changed from searching a range to checking one figure, and the old spread is itself evidence — a coupon offering 3 and 7 as equally plausible is one whose author did not know the answer to within 4 mm, and the rig was built at 7.

One knock-on, recorded because it is the same class of fault: **the vent slots' Z is derived from the chassis cut now**, not declared. At a declared 33.0 with the cut at 31.0 they held 2.0 mm of margin, and moving the module plane 0.9 mm back spent all of it — the slots' gable points reached back over the joint, and the gate caught them in the wrong half. What a slot needs is a stated clearance from the cut, not a Z of its own.

**Shutter switch, terminals and leads.** *Added 2026-09-08 from the first bench print.* The switch was modelled as a plain 6 × 6 × 4.3 mm block with both leads leaving the same face. Neither is true of the switch fitted: its terminals stand proud of the body, and the two leads leave at **opposite ends**. So the retainer's cradle — a 6.6 mm square socket with one notch in it — clamped the terminals, and one lead had nowhere to run but back across the switch. Three changes, and one of them is a decision not to measure: the cradle gains a **terminal slot** through both side walls at the switch's base plane, 4.6 mm wide × 1.8 mm tall, with a 0.6 mm dish in the stop plate under it for legs bent back rather than out — run clean through the wall it passes terminals of any span up to the cup's Ø14.4, so no leg span has to be known, and the 1 mm corner lands each side still locate the switch in that axis. It gains a **second wire notch** opposite the first, because the retainer is a round plate the assembler can seat at any rotation and relieving one side only worked if that happened to be the lead's side. And the pod gains **one lead channel off each end of the switch pocket** — a Ø2 lead in a 3.0 mm gabled channel to the open bay — beside the existing pair that run under the switch. Sideways rather than above: a mirrored channel above the switch would put its gable apex at Z 31.2, through the chassis cut at 31.0 and into the rear half's bed face, where sideways it stays inside the pod's 19 mm width with 6 mm to spare. The slot is 4.6 and not the cradle's full 6.6 so its roof stays a bridge the layer gate allows; measured on the shipped button the widest bridge is 3.90 mm. Four gates: a lead out of each end of the pocket into the bay, clear passage out through both cradle walls at the base plane, and 1.0 mm of stop plate left under the dish. The leg BAND height, 1.8 mm, is the one figure taken from the 6 × 6 tactile family rather than from a caliper on the switch fitted and is marked PROVISIONAL in the source. **Reprint `coupons/KINO_SHUTTER_COUPON.stl` before the front half.**

**Other geometry.** Shutter moved from Z 24 to Z 21 so its Ø17.5 round collar clears the split; pod reach 6 → 3 mm and the pod split into a deep front section and a shallow rear rail-carrier. Light stud: plain Ø8.5 bore through the 6 mm top wall at X 66, Z 16; the 18 mm base plate seats on the flat top; 13.0 mm of stud inside for two 6 mm nuts. Cable exit: 14 × 20 rounded slot at X 40. RESET needle hole removed. Bay 14.6 mm, sized by the straight USB-C plug (16 mm, less the 1.4 mm module margin); the 54 × 33 mm hub board floats in it (12.6 mm of board and plugged headers in 16.0), between the two post rows.

**Lettering and print transforms.** Text is laid out toward −X in body coordinates, which is the reader's right for a viewer facing the camera. The face and cover print transforms are rigid 180° rotations, so the printed object is the model. Two gates: layout direction in the model, and letter direction sliced from the print-frame cover (the O is the glyph with a hole).

**Camera numbers and the foam template.** `1`–`4` are engraved 0.4 mm into the plate's inner face at Y 62, 1.5 mm above each board's top edge, in the same 4 mm typeface: a node is identified by looking into the open bay rather than counted across, and the number still shows with a board fitted. That face points up in the front half's print, so the recesses are free; each is gated to sit on bare plate with nothing standing in the 3 mm above it, and above its own board's edge. The rigs inherit them, their plate being a chunk of this one. Separately, `coupons/KINO_DOOR_FOAM_TEMPLATE.pdf` is a 1:1 cutting template for the door's foam gasket — two cut lines giving a ring 5.26 mm wide at the sides and 3.25 top and bottom, its inner edge on the door's window and its outer 0.75 mm inside the module's outline, with the module outline and the active area drawn as keep-clears — so the strip is cut once and right instead of eyeballed. A gate holds the ring's bearing width, its containment in the module and its clearance from the active area.

**Finish: wall flutes, seam, grain, spec line.** Four changes whose only purpose is that the camera does not advertise how it was made.

(0) **The walls.** Both chassis halves print with Z straight up, so all four outer walls were vertical walls whose only finish was the layer line — measured off the released meshes, 88 % of everything the eye sees on the rear half and 72 % on the front, about 330 cm² wrapping the whole camera, against the 91 cm² of face plate that had the grain. The largest untreated show surface on the object by a factor of three, and the one a hand is on. They now carry a 45° V, 0.25 mm deep and 0.5 wide at the surface on a 1.6 mm pitch — the face plate's grain by eye, so the body reads as one finish — running **along Z**, straight up in both prints: a vertical groove in a vertical wall has no roof, no bridge and no overhang, and a texture hides layer lines best when it runs across them. 267 flutes on a 1.5976 mm step divided into the 426.5 mm loop, so the pattern closes on itself rather than leaving a double line down one corner. Two bands, inset from the front half's bed face, from the seam V on both sides and from the door plane; a 2 mm land around every engraving, opening, foot pad, the strap lug, and the light's 18 mm base seat. Front half 135.07 cm³, rear 77.74, plate 1 212.81; the outer perimeter grows about 18 %.

A round cutter was tried first and withdrawn: it must be tangent to the wall somewhere along its arc, and 267 tangential Booleans a band left two degenerate triangles in the released rear half — then fading the ends out with a clip that crosses every flute at 14° made that 1027 degenerate triangles and 898 non-manifold edges. Square-ended V grooves leave 137.6 mm² of downward face in 472 regions on the front half and 100.6 in 396 on the rear, all of it 0.5 mm bridges that the printability gates read as bridges.

Three things the flutes exposed, each a defect that predated them. Sizing the light mount's land off its Ø8.5 bore instead of its 18 mm base plate took that seat from 82 % to 74 % solid, caught by the light-mount gate. The traced glyph outlines carried pairs of points tens of nanometres apart, and an 8.2e-5 mm edge is a triangle that collapses when the STL is written in float32 — the rear half shipped with two degenerate triangles on the serial's lettering; the outlines are now cleaned in 2-D before they are ever a solid. And two release gates were measuring the wrong quantity: the reassembly gate compared volumes and reported 10.5 mm³ lost alongside **minus** 0.021 mm³ added, which is not a volume a solid can have — it was reading the µm film every shared face leaves — so it now measures mean thickness (2V/A); and the plate-versus-singles gate's flat 0.5 mm³ tolerance was true of a smooth chassis, and is now area × the float32 quantum, because what a quantised surface loses goes as its area.

Elephant-foot relief on the bed faces was built and withdrawn. It is the one edge a moulded part never has, and it lands on the door's show face, the shell's glue joint and the cover's own running face inside 0.10 mm of clearance — but a stepped relief puts a horizontal downward ledge under every step, and a swept one hulled between two rounded rectangles of different radius produces slivers in every file that carries it. Both attempts and the approach that will work (`CrossSection.extrude`'s scaleTop, lofted past both ends of the band it carves) are recorded in the generator. (a) The halves met in a 442 mm butt line round the body at Z 31 — two first layers touching. Each half's outer edge is now chamfered 0.6 mm deep at 50°, so the line sits in a 1.2 × 1.43 mm V-groove and reads as a drawn parting line; a tenth of the wall, and the bolt's head pocket still stops 2.25 mm short of that face. 50° rather than 45° because the rear half prints cut-face-down: at 45 the slice epsilons produced 44.5°, shallower than the rule, and 426 mm² of overhang appeared immediately. The groove is cut before the strap lug is added, so the lug bridges it instead of hanging over it. (b) The face shell's plate — the top surface of that print, where layer lines show worst — carries a linear grain at the stow hatch's 45°: 0.5 mm grooves on a 1.6 mm pitch, 0.2 deep, 31 % of the top 0.2 mm, 1.1 mm lands. Clean lands of 4 mm inside the outline, 2.5 around the lens bar and 2 around the serial are gated solid. Built as one cross-section of rotated quads and extruded once. About 8 m of extra perimeter, roughly five minutes. (c) A spec line, `KINO D4 FIELD BODY`, in 3 mm caps above the serial on the front half's bottom wall, carrying the same four gates as every other mark.

**Gates.** 370 release gates in the generator, throwing on first failure; new ones cover the FOV, lettering direction (twice), split joint (pegs, sockets, pilots, head access, driver path, reassembly as two set statements), cover (fit, retention, coverage, clearance, detent placement, print), foot-pad containment, RESET absence, door window vs active area and module outline, frame evenness, the wordmark (balance, fit, outline-not-grid, thinnest stroke) and the identity marks (23). `overhangArea` now excludes only triangles lying *in* the bed plane. A new **`verify-release.mjs`** re-imports the released STLs through Manifold's own importer, inverts each print transform, and runs 52 independent checks — assembly interference by per-piece film thickness, pegs in sockets, module and standoff clearance, bores and cells located by slicing (pitch 22.000, Ø7.100, coaxial to <0.05 mm), line of sight past the open cover, letter direction, and per-part print readiness.

**Soft edges, cable groove, vents, thumb ridge, wake-on-open.** The face shell's front edge and the rear half's rear rim carry an R3 quarter round (a hull of six insetting slices; both edges face up-and-out in their print orientations; sides stay square). A 4.5 × 2.0 mm groove runs from the USB-C exit slot to the rear edge so a plugged camera sits flat on its 2.2 mm feet — 2.0 deep because the door's dovetail bites 2.0 into the same wall from inside, and a gate holds a 2.0 mm web between them. Four 2 × 11 mm vent slots through each side wall at Y 26/38/50/62, Z 33–45, ahead of the module face and inside the rear half, gabled at both ends so no end face is a ceiling. A 22 × 1.6 mm thumb ridge on the cover stands 0.6 mm proud of the bar. A Hall switch (TO-92S) sits in a Ø4.5 × 5.5 pocket cut from the face's back at X 107.5, Y 34.5 — under the cover's closed position, 1.1 mm of floor to the track — with its lead in a 2.5 mm gabled channel down the face's back to Y 20 and through a Ø2.4 hole in the plate there, the one spot clear of every module PCB and XIAO board; a Ø3 × 1 N52 disc sits flush in the cover's back at the same XY. Magnet present = closed = sleep, absent = open = wake; 22.5 mm of travel separates the states. The sensor's GPIO is on a XIAO — JP1 has no free P4 pin (ECN-0003) — and the firmware for it is not part of this change.

**Glued face shell, aligned through the lens bores.** The face shell is glued to the chassis, not screwed: the four M2 × 8 corner pilots are gone from the shell and their clearance holes from the plate (one sat under the lens bar's chamfered corner and read as a covered-up hole). Alignment comes from the four Ø7.1 lens bores, which run through both parts coaxially on the 22 mm pitch. `KINO_FACE_ALIGN_TOOL.stl` (85 × 14 × 19 mm, 9.0 cm³, one part) is a 6 mm bar with four Ø6.9 pegs on that pitch and a Ø3 lanyard hole at each end; pushed in from the front through the open cells with the cover off, the pegs register in the shell's Ø7.1 land and 5 mm into the plate's bores at 0.10 mm per side, their tips stopping 1.4 mm into the empty cavity. Gates prove the seated tool touches neither shell nor chassis, the fit, the reach, and that the bar clears the track's lips by 0.8 mm. The front plate's openings are now the four bores and the Hall lead hole only; the front-plate void count gate expects six. The Hall sensor must be fitted before gluing — the joint is permanent.

**The KINO D4 wordmark on the cover.** The bitmap-font `KINO` is replaced by the brand wordmark, traced from `docs/assets/brand/kino-d4-black-on-light.png` by `hardware/cad/KINO_FIELD_BODY/brand/trace-mark.py` to **vector outlines**: the raster upsampled four times (bicubic) to 0.0175 mm per pixel, boundary contours smoothed with a 9-point moving average (0.16 mm) and simplified to 0.01 mm — 12 polygons, 472 vertices at 80.0 × 10.7 mm, extruded from a 2-D cross-section. Two earlier cuts were rejected by the maintainer in the slicer: a 0.45 mm pixel-grid trace as blocky, then a pixel-edge trace at 0.07 mm simplified to 0.05 as still faceted (0.9 mm chords on the round letters). The thinnest stroke, the D4 box outline, measures 0.46 mm — one extrusion line, gated at 0.45. Raised 0.6 mm, 0.2 mm proud of the bar, centred in the band between the thumb ridge and the cover's top edge. The trace (`brand/kino-d4-mark.json`) is a derivation of reserved artwork: `REUSE.toml` places `hardware/cad/KINO_FIELD_BODY/brand/**` under `LicenseRef-KINO-Reserved`, and the generator reads it at build time rather than embedding it in CERN-OHL-S source. Direction is gated by ink balance — the trace's left and right thirds hold 768 and 517 cells on a 0.45 mm grid, and the print-frame slice must show the same imbalance the same way round (measured 1.45 against 1.49) — in the generator and again in `verify-release.mjs` on the shipped file; a third gate checks the mark is an outline and not a grid.

**Identity engravings, badge and hatch.** Every part with room carries the design version, read from `hardware/manifest.json` at build time, and a build serial (`buildSerial`, 1): `REV 0.1.4 · SN 001`, 45.1 × 4.3 mm, engraved 0.4 mm deep in **Roboto Regular** at 4.0 mm caps, traced to outlines by `fonts/trace-font.py` into `fonts/roboto-regular.json` (Apache-2.0, declared in `REUSE.toml`; no font software ships) and emboldened 0.08 mm a side; a gate erodes the outline by half a nozzle line and re-dilates, and under 1 % of the ink fails to come back. Three earlier lettering cuts were rejected by the maintainer: 5 × 7 pixel glyphs as blocky, then a hand-drawn italic stroke font of 0.6 mm capsules as too basic. Front half: bottom wall at X 65.5, Z 27. Rear half: bottom at X 88, Z 47 (at X 81 the longer line reached the right zip-tie slot). Face shell: front, above the lens bar at Y 68 (under it, at Y 12, the recess landed inside the turned-over bar). Cover: its back at Y 44. Door: outer face at Y 7, below the window's bevel (a first placement at Y 8.5 ran into the bevel and failed two gates). Alignment tool: the face you hold. The clamps and shutter button carry none. The chassis' top wall carries the wordmark's own boxed `D4` as a badge — the six polygons of the box cut from the brand trace, scaled to 9.0 mm tall (25.3 wide, outline 0.6 mm) — at X 90, Z 24, between the light bore and the shutter collar. The face's stow zone (Y 55.5–75.3, the track floor the open cover uncovers) is hatched with 0.6 mm grooves on a 2.5 mm pitch at 45°, 0.3 mm deep; the floor under the closed cover is gated 100 % solid. Twenty-three gates: each mark cut (residue under 3 % of its recess), reading the right way round (the part brought back into the text's frame, ink in left and right thirds compared with the text's own outline), material behind it; hatch density; closed zone unhatched. ## Safety and recovery

No electrical effect. The hub board floats unmounted in the bay by decision; the maintainer wraps it in Kapton. Foam for the door must be **thicker than the 1.2 mm gap** (about 2 mm stock) or the module has no preload. Two inputs are still assumptions and are named as such in the CAD README: the active area centred on the PCB (0.8 mm/side of window margin depends on it) and the 78° field. If a printed part fails, nothing but the part is affected; a failed half costs at most 6.2 h.
